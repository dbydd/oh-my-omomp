# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English

Working rule set for controlled technical English. Condensed from ASD-STE100 Issue 9
(January 2025). This page paraphrases the public rules. It does not reproduce the
standard or its dictionary.

Use this page for skills, runbooks, procedures, reference docs, commit messages, and
any text that a reader must understand without asking a question. For papers and
general prose, keep the voice rules in `SKILL.md` and apply these rules where they fit.

## Why the rules exist

ASD-STE100 is a controlled language. Aviation maintenance crews needed documentation
that a non-native reader cannot misread. The reader has no back-channel to the author.

An agent reading a skill, a tool description, or a handoff sits in the same position.
The reader cannot ask what a pronoun points to. Use these rules to remove that risk.

## Rule map

| Section | Subject | Core rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Words | One word, one meaning, one part of speech |
| 2 | Multi-word nouns | Maximum 3 words per noun cluster |
| 3 | Verbs | Simple tenses only, active voice |
| 4 | Sentences | One idea per sentence, no omitted words |
| 5 | Procedures | Maximum 20 words, imperative form |
| 6 | Descriptions | Maximum 25 words, one topic per paragraph |
| 7 | Safety | State the command and the consequence |
| 8 | Punctuation | No semicolon |
| 9 | Style | Rewrite the sentence, keep one term per object |

## 1. Words

- Use one word for one meaning. Use it as one part of speech only.
- Use a word in its approved sense only. `follow` means `come after`. Write `obey`
  when you mean `obey`.
- Pick the short common word. Drop the formal synonym.
- Use a technical noun when the domain needs it. Define the term at first use.
- Keep one term for one object across the whole document. Never vary the name for
  style.
- Use a verb for an action. Write `the meter shows 450 ohms`, not `the meter gives
  an indication of 450 ohms`.
- Use one spelling variety throughout. Never change text inside a quotation.

## 2. Multi-word nouns

- Keep a noun cluster to 3 words or fewer. Write `runway light connection`. Avoid
  `runway light connection resistance calibration`.
- Rephrase a long cluster with prepositions. Write `calibration of the resistance
  of the runway light connection`.
- Write an official long term in full at first use. Then give a short form and keep
  it, or hyphenate the words that act as one unit.

## 3. Verbs

- Use these forms only: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple
  future, and past participle as an adjective.
- Use no perfect tense and no progressive tense. Write `we received`, not `we have
  received`.
- Use no compound auxiliary construction. Avoid `has been`, `will have been`,
  `is to be`, `must be` plus a past participle.
- Use `-ing` only as a technical noun or as part of one. Write `the grinding wheel`.
  Use no `-ing` verb form and no long `-ing` modifier clause.
- Use active voice in procedures and instructions. Use passive voice in a
  description only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- A past participle as an adjective is not passive voice. `The unit is fully
  disassembled` is correct.

## 4. Sentences

- Write short, clear, concrete sentences. Use the imperative form for an instruction.
- Never drop a word to shorten a sentence. Keep the subject, the verb, and the
  article. Write no contractions.
- Use a vertical list for several items or steps. Put a colon before the list.
  Capitalize each item. Keep one marker style. Keep all items at the same level.
- Link related sentences with plain connectors: `and`, `then`, `thus`, `as a result`.
- Use `the`, `a`, and `an` before nouns. Omit an article only in a general statement
  about an abstract concept.

## 5. Procedures and instructions

- Use a maximum of 20 words per sentence.
- Put one instruction in one sentence. Combine two actions only when they happen at
  the same time.
- Start with the imperative verb. Write `Remove the four screws`, not `The four
  screws should be removed`.
- State a condition first, then the command, separated by a comma. Write `If the
  pump runs, turn it off`.
- A note gives information only. Never put an instruction in a note.

## 6. Descriptions

- Present the general case first. Add one layer of detail at a time.
- Repeat the key terms to keep one logical thread.
- Use a maximum of 25 words per sentence.
- Use one paragraph for one topic. Use a maximum of 6 sentences per paragraph.

## 7. Safety instructions

- Use `WARNING` for a risk of injury or death. Use `CAUTION` for a risk of damage to
  equipment or data. Use `WARNING` when both risks apply.
- Start with the command or with the condition that starts the risk.
- State the consequence of a failure to obey.

## 8. Punctuation and word count

- Use standard punctuation. Use no semicolon. Write two sentences or use a
  connector.
- Use parentheses for a text reference, an identifier, a step number, an
  abbreviation, or a short explanation.
- Count these as one word each: a number, a unit, an abbreviation, an identifier, a
  quoted string, a title, and a proper noun.
- Count a hyphenated compound as one word. Count a parenthetical aside as one word.

## 9. Style and construction

- Rewrite the whole sentence when a word swap reads badly. Do not force an
  ungrammatical substitute.
- Use a word only in the sense that is correct in that context.
- Avoid a phrasal verb. Write `extinguish the fire`, not `put out the fire`.
- Reuse the exact term and the exact phrasing for a repeated action. Do not
  paraphrase for variety.

## Habits beyond the rules

- Keep the conjunction `that` after `make sure`, `show`, and `recommend`.
- Reword a sentence when `with` can mean association, means, or accompaniment.
- Repeat the noun when a pronoun has more than one possible antecedent.
- Write `for example` and `that is`. Avoid `e.g.`, `i.e.`, and `etc.`
- Use gender-neutral language.
- Rephrase a possessive when the sense is unclear.

## Self-check before delivery

1. Split every sentence longer than the limit for its type.
2. Rewrite every passive sentence that has a known actor.
3. Convert every `-ing` verb form to a simple tense.
4. Give one object one name.
5. Add the consequence to every warning and caution.
6. Make the list markers, the capitalization, and the item level consistent.
7. Remove every semicolon, contraction, and dropped article.
8. Break every noun cluster longer than 3 words.

## Scope in this skill set

- Skills, procedures, and reference docs: apply every rule on this page.
- Reports and chat replies: apply sections 1 to 4, 8, and 9.
- Papers and other authored prose: keep the source voice. Apply this page to a
  procedure, a caption, or a definition inside the paper.
